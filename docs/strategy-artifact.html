FGN/Creator Platform/Build vs Buy

The Recruitment Wedge

We set out to replicate Sideqik. The market research says that for generic influencer marketing, building is indefensible. But FGN is not doing generic influencer marketing, and the part it is doing cannot be bought.

The finding that changes the decision

Licensing the data costs three times more than buying the finished product

$16,200
Modash, per year
Raw creator discovery data. An API, not a product.
$6,000
Aspire or Upfluence, per year
A complete mid-market platform. All eight pillars, finished.
$30k–39k
CreatorIQ, per year
Enterprise entry. Six figures at scale.

If the goal were generic influencer marketing, the answer would be to buy Aspire and stop building. More expensive on data alone, plus the entire engineering cost, to end up behind. That is the honest verdict, and it is worth stating plainly before the argument for building.

Verdict

Do not rebuild the category. Build the wedge, buy or defer the commodity.

The saved searches give the real business away. Farm Simulator Challenge and Construction Simulator Challenge recruit creators into FGN challenges. HCTC Texas Gamers and Arizona Minecraft Players are geo-bounded recruitment inside ISP partner territories. No commercial platform can answer “find Minecraft creators living in ZIP codes served by HCTC.” CreatorIQ cannot. GRIN cannot. Sideqik could not either, which is why those searches settled for state-level filters.

FGN already owns the missing half: tenant_zip_codes, national_zip_codes, lookup_providers_by_zip and the multi-tenant ISP model in play.fgn.gg. The thing no vendor sells is the thing FGN already built.

Where the market is moving in 2026

Compensation is shifting from flat fees to performance, with three to six month partnerships and creators treated as affiliate partners. Social commerce is the engine: TikTok Shop is projected at $23.4 billion in US ecommerce sales this year, up 48 percent. Creator licensing, previously called whitelisting, has become a standard line item.

Measurement is consolidating away from EMV. The criticisms are now mainstream: no industry-standard calculation, it ignores audience quality, and a high EMV does not prove revenue impact. 79 percent of marketers cite measuring influencer ROI as their biggest obstacle. Sideqik showed FGN $0 earned media value and Share of Voice was never enabled, so FGN never used that layer. Rebuilding it would mean rebuilding a metric the market is abandoning and FGN never touched.

Why FGN is a different business

Recruitment bounded by service territory, not brand campaigns bounded by budget

The wedge is narrow, defensible, and it is the only part of this worth building. Everything else is either commodity we can buy or scope we should decline.

Explicit non-goals. EMV, Share of Voice, TikTok Shop, affiliate links, product seeding, UGC rights, e-signature, and hand-rolled payments. These are the guardrail against scope creeping back toward a full platform clone. Revisit them deliberately, not by drift.

Open decisions

One question gates the rest

Do you accept the wedge framing? If FGN’s creator work is really generic brand-influencer marketing, the honest answer is to buy Aspire at roughly $6,000 a year and stop building. If it is ISP-territory creator recruitment into challenges, no vendor sells it and building is right. Everything below follows from that answer.

The risk worth naming. The wedge could be a story we tell ourselves. Phase 3 is the test: if ISP-territory filtering does not change which creators actually get recruited, the wedge is not real and buying becomes the right answer. That failure is cheap and visible fast, which is the main argument for sequencing it early.
